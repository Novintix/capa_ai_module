STABILITY AND QUALITY ASSESSMENT REPORT
Report Number: SR-2026-0112
Product: Smart Infusion Set with Anti-Free-Flow Clamp (Model IFX-20)
Batch: IS-517
Legacy Evidence Tag: M-517
Issued By: QC Device Quality Group
Issue Date: 2026-03-02

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Batch IS-517 showed elevated reject count for translucent micro-fragments inside the fluid path and intermittent flow restriction. Functional dimensions and sealing integrity remained within specification for non-defective samples. Strongest correlation is with process-governance and restart-control failure after maintenance.

2. PURPOSE
Consolidate visual, flow-performance, microscopy, and trend data to support formal investigation and CAPA decisions.

3. SAMPLE PLAN AND METHODS
3.1 Sample selection
- Final inspection rejects and retains sampled across beginning, middle, and end of run.
3.2 Visual and borescope analysis
- Controlled station visual plus borescope inspection for inner channel defects.
3.3 Functional tests
- Flow-rate verification and anti-free-flow clamp response testing.
3.4 Material characterization
- FTIR and microscopy for particle composition and morphology.

4. RESULTS
4.1 Defect findings
- Rejected units: 96 / 8,000 (1.20 percent).
- Primary defect: translucent polymer-like micro-fragments in fluid path.
- Secondary defect: intermittent flow restriction under standard test pressure.

4.2 Functional indicators
- Non-defective sample flow rates: within target.
- Seal integrity: acceptable in non-defective units.

4.3 Trend context
- Previous four lots had reject rates between 0.22 and 0.39 percent.
- IS-517 exceeded action threshold 0.70 percent.

5. MORPHOLOGY SUMMARY
- Predominant fragment shape: thin translucent slivers, 80-280 micrometers.
- Secondary observation: small polymer flecks near lane-B guide region.
- Preliminary source type: process-contact polymer abrasion rather than ambient contamination.

6. CORRELATION WITH PROCESS EVIDENCE
- Restart occurred before witness sign-off completion.
- Lot-reconciliation warning unresolved at initial restart.
- Open critical item verbally transferred at shift boundary.
- Throughput recovery pressure documented in assembly log.

7. RISK INTERPRETATION
Quality Risk: High
Compliance Risk: High
Patient Risk: Medium-High (IV use-case; risk managed via quarantine)

8. RECOMMENDATIONS
Immediate:
1. Maintain quarantine and execute 100 percent reinspection of IS-517.
2. Continue source confirmation with guide insert and tubing contact-point analysis.
3. Enforce witness and lot-reconciliation dual gate before all restarts.

Near-term:
1. Deploy MES dual-lock for witness and lot reconciliation.
2. Implement mandatory signed handover for open quality-critical items.
3. Open CAPA for governance and effectiveness-review methodology gaps.

9. LIMITATIONS
- Some source attribution confidence depends on completion of remaining comparative samples.
- Human-factor evidence based on records/interviews requires management-system corroboration.

10. CONCLUSION
The event is most consistent with restart-governance failure and traceability-control bypass in a post-maintenance context, not broad process drift or environmental contamination.

Interpretation:
- The event lot behavior is not random variation around historical process performance.
- Statistical signal supports assignment to special-cause conditions.
