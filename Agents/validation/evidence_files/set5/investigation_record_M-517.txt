1. INCIDENT DESCRIPTION
During 100 percent final visual and flow verification, 96 units out of 8,000 were rejected for translucent micro-fragment particles inside the fluid path and intermittent flow restriction. Reject ratio exceeded internal action limit and triggered formal investigation.

2. INVESTIGATION OBJECTIVES
- Determine direct and contributing causes for particulate and flow restriction defects.
- Assess potential patient impact for IV infusion use-case.
- Verify whether post-maintenance restart controls and DHR traceability controls were followed.
- Define containment and long-term CAPA recommendations.

3. SCOPE

- Shift handover records and barcode lot reconciliation logs.

8. CAUSAL ANALYSIS
8.1 Primary Cause
- Failure to enforce mandatory post-maintenance release control before restart on a sterile fluid-path assembly line.

8.2 Contributing Cause 1
- Traceability control bypass: unresolved lot reconciliation warning acknowledged without correction.

8.3 Contributing Cause 2
- Inadequate handover governance for unresolved quality-critical item.

8.4 Contributing Cause 3
- Throughput-biased operational pressure and delayed deviation initiation.

8.5 Contributing Cause 4
- CAPA effectiveness method relied on activity completion rather than confirming technical gate deployment.

9. RISK ASSESSMENT
Severity: High (device fluid path used for intravenous delivery)
Occurrence: Medium (similar process-control failures seen in prior records)
Detectability: Medium (defects detected at final inspection, not prevented at source)
Initial Risk Level: High

10. CONTAINMENT ACTIONS
- Quarantine all 8,000 units from Batch IS-517.
- Expanded 100 percent borescope visual and flow-rate verification on affected lot.
- Temporary rule: QA pre-assigned witness for all post-maintenance restarts on ASM lines.
- Mandatory signed DHR handover for any open quality-critical item.

11. PROPOSED CAPA PLAN
CAPA-1: MES lock to prevent RUN status if FRM-ASM-109 witness field is blank.
CAPA-2: Auto-block release when barcode lot reconciliation warning remains unresolved.
CAPA-3: Supervisor KPI update to include compliance hold quality metric (minimum 30 percent weighting).
CAPA-4: Scenario-based retraining focused on stop authority under dispatch pressure.
CAPA-5: Monthly recurrence review with QA, Manufacturing, Engineering, and Regulatory Affairs.

12. EFFECTIVENESS CRITERIA
- 100 percent post-maintenance restarts with witness sign-off prior to RUN.
- Zero unresolved barcode lot reconciliation warnings at restart for 3 consecutive months.
- Zero verbal-only transfer of open critical items for 3 consecutive months.
- At least 90 percent reduction in delayed critical entries within two quarters.

14. SEVEN-WHY SYSTEMIC CHAIN
WHY-1: Why were micro-fragments and intermittent flow restrictions found?
Because debris entered or remained in fluid-path channels after restart and controls were not fully completed.

WHY-2: Why was fluid path not fully verified before restart?
Because line restart occurred prior to completion of post-maintenance verification and witness sign-off.

WHY-3: Why did restart occur with incomplete verification?
Because system allowed HOLD to RUN transition without validating FRM-ASM-109 witness completion and unresolved lot-mapping warning.

WHY-4: Why did supervisors allow continuation despite known gaps?
Because no hard system lock existed and schedule pressure incentivized continuation with intent to close records later.

WHY-5: Why was hard lock not in place despite prior CAPA commitments?
Because IT reprioritization delayed CR-IT-2025-188 and delay was not accompanied by formal risk acceptance.

WHY-6: Why did management review not escalate prolonged delay?
Because review template tracked status labels but lacked risk-tier trigger criteria for overdue quality-critical controls.

WHY-7: Why did governance framework fail to protect compliance controls during project reprioritization?
Because cross-functional prioritization lacked mandatory QA approval and formal risk documentation for quality-critical deferrals.
