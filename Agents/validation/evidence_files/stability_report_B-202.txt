STABILITY AND QUALITY ASSESSMENT REPORT
Report Number: SR-2026-0041
Product: Ceftriaxone for Injection 1g (Vial)
Batch: CF-882
Legacy Evidence Tag: B-202
Issued By: QC Stability Group
Issue Date: 2026-02-15

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Batch CF-882 exhibited an elevated final visual reject count for white lint-like particulate findings. Chemical quality indicators remained within specification. Evidence suggests the strongest linkage is process-governance and documentation-control weakness around post-maintenance restart and handover controls.

2. PURPOSE
This report consolidates quality signals from visual inspection, trend analysis, retained sample assessments, and record review for use in formal investigation and CAPA decisions.

3. SAMPLE PLAN AND METHODS
3.1 Batch and retain sample selection
- Finished units sampled according to AQL-based final visual plan.
- Retains selected from beginning, middle, and end of run.
3.2 Visual inspection
- Controlled booth inspection by certified inspectors.
- Defect categorization by morphology.
3.3 Chemical quality checks
- Assay and related substances review from release and retain data.
3.4 Ancillary review
- Process chronology and documentation status cross-check from manufacturing records.

4. RESULTS
4.1 Visual Defect Findings
- Rejected units: 142 / 10,000.
- Primary observed defect: white lint-like particulate appearance.
- Defect pattern distribution:
  - First third of run: lower frequency.
  - Middle third: moderate frequency.
  - Final third: highest concentration.
4.2 Chemical and potency indicators
- Assay: within approved specification.
- Related substances: within approved specification.
- pH and reconstitution checks: within specification.
4.3 Trend context
- Prior three campaign lots did not show equivalent particulate spike.
- This event exceeds historical mean and action trend threshold.

5. DEFECT MORPHOLOGY SUMMARY
- Predominant morphology: thin filamentous white fibers.
- Secondary morphology: occasional short fragmented opaque particles.
- Preliminary interpretation: likely extrinsic source contribution; final material characterization pending.

6. CORRELATION WITH PROCESS AND DOCUMENTATION EVIDENCE
- Maintenance-triggered restart event documented.
- Clear-Clean-Check record indicates delayed completion and missing witness sign-off at runtime.
- Open compliance item transferred verbally at shift boundary.
- Throughput recovery pressure documented in operations log.
- No strong evidence of formulation instability as primary driver.

7. RISK INTERPRETATION
Quality Risk: Elevated
Compliance Risk: Elevated
Patient Risk: Pending final particulate composition, currently assessed as Medium with precautionary controls.

8. RECOMMENDATIONS
Immediate:
1. Maintain quarantine and expanded visual reinspection.
2. Complete particulate characterization with external microscopy if needed.
3. Enforce pre-restart witness requirement for post-maintenance interventions.
Near-term:
1. Implement system control preventing restart when witness field blank.
2. Reinforce written handover discipline for open quality items.
3. Open CAPA focused on governance behavior and control effectiveness.

9. LIMITATIONS
- Final particulate source attribution requires laboratory characterization completion.
- Some operational evidence is based on logbook narrative and requires corroboration with system audit trails.

10. CONCLUSION
Batch CF-882 quality event is most consistent with process execution and documentation-governance failure around maintenance restart controls rather than chemical instability. Investigation should prioritize accountability, restart gating, and handover controls.
