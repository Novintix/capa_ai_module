STANDARD OPERATING PROCEDURE
Title: Controlled Line Clearance and Clear-Clean-Check After Maintenance Interventions
SOP Number: SOP-MFG-044
Version: 4.3
Effective Date: 2025-03-15
Supersedes: SOP-MFG-044 v4.2
Department: Manufacturing Operations

1. PURPOSE
Define mandatory activities to assure product-contact line readiness after maintenance interventions, and prevent contamination risks associated with incomplete clearance.

2. SCOPE
Applies to all filling, transfer, and product-contact support lines where maintenance opens or disturbs closed process pathways.

3. REFERENCES
- POL-QA-005 Deviation Management and Escalation
- POL-QA-012 Good Documentation Practice
- POL-MFG-031 Shift Handover Governance
- FRM-MFG-022 Clear-Clean-Check
- FRM-MFG-031 Shift Handover Form
- FRM-QA-DEV-01 Deviation Record

4. DEFINITIONS
- Clear-Clean-Check: Structured post-maintenance verification of cleanliness, readiness, and record completeness.
- Witness: Authorized QA or delegate who confirms checklist completion.
- Hold State: Operational lockout where processing cannot continue without release.

5. ROLE RESPONSIBILITIES
5.1 Operator
- Execute checklist in real time.
- Record findings and abnormalities.
- Do not resume processing without completed checklist and witness sign-off.
5.2 Maintenance Technician
- Confirm intervention complete and area free of tools or temporary parts.
- Sign work order closure details.
5.3 QA Witness
- Verify checklist evidence and line condition.
- Sign witness section before release.
5.4 Shift Supervisor
- Confirm no open critical record before shift handover and restart.

6. TRIGGER EVENTS REQUIRING FULL CLEAR-CLEAN-CHECK
- Pump-head replacement
- Product-contact hose disconnection/replacement
- Valve seal replacement in product path
- Nozzle or manifold disassembly
- Any maintenance opening sterile boundary in processing path

7. PRE-RESTART CONTROL FLOW
Step 1: Set line status HOLD.
Step 2: Verify intervention closure with maintenance signature.
Step 3: Perform physical inspection against FRM-MFG-022 sections.
Step 4: Record completion timestamps and operator initials.
Step 5: Obtain QA witness signature.
Step 6: Supervisor verifies attached checklist in batch packet.
Step 7: Release HOLD and resume operation.

8. CHECKLIST EXECUTION DETAIL
8.1 Area and Accessory Cleanliness
- Confirm no lint, residues, fibers, or loose packaging debris.
- Confirm all cleaning labels are current.
8.2 Product-Contact Path Integrity
- Confirm fittings locked and seals seated.
- Confirm no temporary tools or parts remain.
8.3 Documentation Completeness
- Confirm form revision current.
- Confirm mandatory fields completed.
8.4 Alarm and Status Review
- Confirm no active unresolved alarms linked to intervention area.

9. DOCUMENTATION RULES
- All entries must be contemporaneous.
- No blank mandatory fields permitted.
- Corrections require GDP-compliant correction method.
- Delayed entry requires documented reason and supervisor approval.

10. SHIFT HANDOVER REQUIREMENT
If checklist remains open at handover:
- Processing remains HOLD unless QA grants documented exception.
- Open item transferred via signed FRM-MFG-031.
- Incoming shift explicitly accepts ownership.

11. DEVIATION TRIGGERS
Immediate deviation required when any of the following occurs:
- Restart prior to witness sign-off.
- Missing checklist attachment in active batch packet.
- Verbal-only transfer of open critical item.
- Post-facto record reconstruction of critical control step.

12. RISK CONTAINMENT GUIDANCE
- Quarantine affected units where restart control failure occurred.
- Expand visual and particulate inspection as directed by QA.
- Stop similar interventions pending briefing if trend indicates repeat issue.

13. TRAINING REQUIREMENTS
- Initial qualification for all operators and supervisors.
- Annual refresher mandatory.
- Additional briefing after critical/major related deviations.

14. EFFECTIVENESS METRICS
- Target: 100 percent post-maintenance restarts with complete witness sign-off before RUN status.
- Target: Zero verbal-only transfers of open critical items for 3 consecutive months.
- Target: No delayed critical documentation entries across 6 consecutive audit cycles.
- Tracking method: Monthly compliance report generated by QA from e-batch packet review.
- Reporting: Results reviewed at monthly Manufacturing Quality Review meeting and escalated if trend falls below target for 2 consecutive periods.

15. MES SYSTEM LOCK REQUIREMENT
15.1 Background and Mandate
Following CAPA-2024-0097, it was identified that human-dependent enforcement of witness sign-off is insufficient as a standalone control. A system-level control was proposed to prevent line release when mandatory checklist fields are blank. This requirement was restated in the SOP as a hard control obligation.
15.2 Required System Behavior
- The Manufacturing Execution System (MES) must prevent operator-initiated transition of line status from HOLD or SETUP to RUN when the witness signature field of FRM-MFG-022 associated with the active batch is unsigned.
- This lock applies to all lines where a maintenance work order of type "Product-Contact Intervention" is linked to the active batch.
- Override capability must require dual QA Director sign-off with documented risk justification; override events must generate automatic deviation ticket.
15.3 Implementation Status (as of SOP effective date)
- MES lock feature was specified in IT Change Request ITR-2024-0443, raised 2024-09-14.
- Projected go-live per IT project plan was 2025-Q1.
- As of SOP-MFG-044 v4.3 (effective 2025-03-15), IT project ITR-2024-0443 is in UAT phase; full deployment not yet confirmed.
- Until system lock is live, compensating control (Section 7 and supervisor sign-off) remains mandatory.
- SOP owner must verify deployment status quarterly and update this section accordingly.
15.4 Risk of Non-Deployment
If system lock is not deployed, residual human error risk around restart control remains. Any batch processed under compensating controls without system gate should be flagged in the batch review meeting.

16. ESCALATION MATRIX FOR RESTART CONTROL FAILURES
Scenario A - Witness unavailable at planned restart window:
  - Action: Delay restart; operator notifies shift supervisor immediately.
  - Supervisor action: Contact QA duty manager to assign alternative witness.
  - Maximum wait allowance: 30 minutes before formal escalation to QA Manager.
  - If wait exceeds 30 minutes without resolution: Document formal hold and notify Production Manager.
Scenario B - Restart occurred before witness sign-off discovered post-hoc:
  - Action: Immediate line HOLD.
  - Operator raises deviation ticket within 30 minutes of discovery.
  - QA Manager notified within 1 hour.
  - Risk assessment of affected product units initiated within 2 hours.
  - Batch disposition held pending investigation.
Scenario C - Open checklist found at shift handover:
  - Action: Mandatory written transfer via FRM-MFG-031; incoming shift accepts ownership in writing.
  - If written transfer not possible: Line remains HOLD; Production Manager contacted.
  - No verbal-only carry-forward permitted under any circumstances.
Scenario D - Verbal handover of open compliance item discovered:
  - Treat as Scenario B above; raise deviation for handover governance failure independent of restart status.
Scenario E - Pattern of repeated escalation at same process step:
  - QA flags for CAPA review; root cause assessment initiated within 5 business days.
  - Management briefing within 10 business days.

17. DETAILED TRAINING AND COMPETENCY REQUIREMENTS
17.1 Role-Based Training Matrix
Operator:
  - Initial: Classroom instruction on SOP-MFG-044, FRM-MFG-022 completion practice, GDP basics.
  - Practical: Supervised execution of at least 3 line clearance events before independent operation.
  - Annual refresher: E-learning module with 80 percent minimum pass score.
  - Post-deviation: Additional practical on-line assessment within 10 business days of related event.
Shift Supervisor:
  - Initial: Full SOP-MFG-044 classroom plus escalation scenario simulation.
  - Practical: Demonstrated decision capability in 2 simulated scenarios including throughput pressure scenario.
  - Annual refresher: Facilitated scenario workshop with QA.
  - Post-deviation: Coaching session with QA Manager on supervisory decision-making under schedule pressure.
QA Witness:
  - Initial: FRM-MFG-022 verification standards, common failure modes, GDP cross-check.
  - Practical: Witness 5 actual clearances under mentor oversight before independent witnessing.
  - Annual: Calibration exercise comparing checklist decisions across QA witnesses.
Maintenance Technician:
  - Initial: Awareness of restart governance obligations when closing work orders on product-contact equipment.
  - Annual: Review of work order closure requirements.
17.2 Training Effectiveness Assessment
- Training completion alone is not sufficient evidence of behavioral compliance.
- Each annual refresher cohort must be followed by a 90-day observation audit checking real-time documentation compliance.
- Observation audit results reported to QA Manager and included in site training effectiveness report.
- If observation audit reveals compliance rate below 90 percent, additional coaching and re-assessment are mandatory.
17.3 Root Cause of Prior Training Inadequacy
Documented in CAPA-2025-0038: GDP training was completed on schedule, but real-time compliance did not improve because training did not address behavioral drivers like throughput pressure and cultural normalization of delayed documentation. Revised training includes scenario-based exercises specifically addressing pressure-to-deviate situations.

18. INTEGRATION WITH MANAGEMENT REVIEW AND QUALITY OVERSIGHT
18.1 Monthly Manufacturing Quality Review
- Restart compliance rate (percent restarts with complete witness sign-off before RUN) reported as standing agenda item.
- Any month with compliance below 98 percent triggers root cause discussion at the review.
- Trend over rolling 12 months tracked and visualized in QA dashboard.
18.2 Site Leadership Escalation Trigger
- Two or more restart control failures in any 30-day period: QA Director presents summary to Site Leadership.
- Any repeat event after CAPA closure: Mandatory Site Leadership review within 15 business days.
- Regulatory observation related to restart governance: Immediate Site Leadership briefing; formal response plan within 30 days.
18.3 Connection to CAPA System
- Any deviation raised under trigger conditions in Section 11 must be cross-linked to this SOP in the CAPA system.
- CAPA effectiveness reviews must verify system control deployment status, not only training completion.
18.4 KPI Governance Alignment
- Production throughput KPIs must not create incentive structures that reward continuation over compliance.
- Site Quality Council conducts annual review of production performance KPIs to confirm compliance behaviors are not penalized.
- This SOP is cited as a reference document in the KPI governance framework (ref: Site Quality Council Charter, QSC-2024-01).
- If any shift or team performance score is negatively impacted by a compliance-mandated line hold, the hold event is scored as a positive compliance action, not a production failure.
18.5 Prior Regulatory Observations
- 2024 regulatory inspection observation (Obs-2024-INS-07): Inspector noted that KPI structures at the site could create implicit pressure to prioritize throughput. Site committed to review KPI weighting within 12 months.
- As of SOP v4.3 effective date, KPI review action was in progress but not yet fully implemented. QA Director tracking as open commitment item.

22. POST-MAINTENANCE VERIFICATION (PMV) PROTOCOL
22.1 Purpose
Define the specific technical steps that constitute a complete post-maintenance verification after a product-contact intervention, separate from but linked to the Clear-Clean-Check documentation requirements in Section 7.

22.2 PMV Scope
Applies after any product-contact intervention including but not limited to: pump-head replacement, seal replacement, hose disconnection, nozzle disassembly, valve component replacement.

22.3 PMV Steps (to be executed by Operator, verified by QA Witness)

Step PMV-1: Physical integrity check.
  - Confirm all wetted-path fittings are torqued to specification and sealed.
  - Confirm transfer hose: no visible kinking, no exterior abrasion at contact points, routing clips correctly positioned.
  - Confirm nozzles: seated, no residue from disassembly.
  - Confirm seals: all seats visible and correct orientation.
  - Sign PMV-1 section of FRM-MFG-022.

Step PMV-2: Pre-flush verification.
  - Conduct timed WFI flush of product-contact path per SOP-MFG-011 Section 8.
  - Collect flush sample (1 L) into particle-counting vessel.
  - If flush particle count exceeds in-process alert limit (>25 particles ≥10 µm per mL), escalate to QA before proceeding.
  - Record flush volume and particle count result in FRM-MFG-022 PMV section.

Step PMV-3: Commissioning run.
  - Run pump P-17 (or equivalent) at specified commissioning flow rate for 5 minutes before connecting to product path.
  - Verify no vibration, no pressure anomaly, no leak at fittings.
  - Record commissioning run confirmation in FRM-MFG-022.

Step PMV-4: QA witness verification.
  - QA Witness reviews PMV-1 through PMV-3 completion.
  - QA Witness performs independent physical observation of at least PMV-1 items.
  - QA Witness signs FRM-MFG-022 witness field (this is the field referenced in SOP Section 7 Step 5).
  - Without this sign-off, line status must not transition to RUN.

22.4 Timed PMV Requirements
- PMV-1: Minimum 10 minutes.
- PMV-2: Flush duration per SOP-MFG-011 + particle count analysis time (approximately 15 minutes total).
- PMV-3: 5 minutes commissioning run + review.
- PMV-4: Concurrent with PMV-1 to PMV-3 where QA is available; if QA is required sequentially, add 5-10 minutes.
- Minimum total PMV time: 30-40 minutes. Must be planned into batch schedule for any batch following a product-contact maintenance event.

22.5 PMV Failure Criteria
- PMV fails if any PMV-1 physical finding indicates product-contact path integrity concern.
- PMV fails if PMV-2 flush particle count exceeds alert limit.
- PMV fails if PMV-3 commissioning run shows anomalous vibration, pressure, or leak.
- PMV failure requires QA escalation. Equipment returns to HOLD. Maintenance review required before re-attempt.

22.6 Planning Obligation
- Batch schedule must include PMV time for any batch following a product-contact maintenance work order.
- Production planning must confirm QA witness availability window no later than 2 hours before planned PMV start.
- If QA witness cannot be confirmed within 2 hours of planned PMV start, batch start must be delayed; Production Manager and QA Manager notified.

23. HOSE AND WETTED-PATH INTEGRITY ASSESSMENT PROCEDURE
23.1 Background
Investigation INV-2026-0019 identified that transfer hose exterior abrasion at routing clip contact points was a probable particulate shed source in Batch CF-882. No prior assessment had evaluated hose routing configurations as a particulate risk vector. This section codifies the required assessment.

23.2 Periodic Hose Inspection (addition to PM scope)
Frequency: Every 90 days or after any hose repositioning, whichever is sooner.
Performed by: Maintenance Technician + QA witness.
Inspection items:
  - Examine full hose length for visible kinking, flattening, surface crazing, or abrasion marks.
  - At each routing clip or support contact point: inspect for pressure marks, white residue (polymer shed indicator), surface breaks.
  - Document inspection against hose map (reference: EQ-QUAL-HOSE-FL02 schematic).
  - Any finding of surface abrasion, crazing, or extensive pressure marks: quarantine hose from service; raise work order for replacement.
  - No finding: sign Hose Inspection Record (FRM-MFG-033) and file with PM records.

23.3 Hose Routing Change Control
Any change to hose routing or clip positioning must be raised as a formal change control under POL-QA-028.
  - Impact assessment must include particulate shed risk evaluation.
  - QA approval required before new routing is used in production.
  - Like-for-like maintenance reclassification does not apply to routing changes; any positional change requires change control.

23.4 Pre-Batch Hose Check (add to FRM-MFG-022 checklist)
Before each batch on a line where a product-contact hose was disturbed during maintenance:
  - Inspect hose exterior for abrasion at all contact points.
  - Confirm no white powder residue at clip zones.
  - If either condition found: do not proceed; raise maintenance work order.
  - Record confirmation on FRM-MFG-022 Section 8.2 (Product-Contact Path Integrity).

24. ALARM RESPONSE ESCALATION PROCEDURE (CLARIFICATION AND EXPANSION)
24.1 Scope of This Section
SOP-MFG-044 Section 8.4 references alarm review. This section provides the explicit response matrix that was absent and contributed to the alarm management gap in Batch CF-882.

24.2 Alarm Response Matrix for Line FL-02

Alarm Type                     | Auto-Recover Possible | Required Action                                      | Escalation
Transfer pressure dip (<0.5s)  | Yes                   | Log entry; confirm physical check at next 15-min     | None if single; QA if >2 in 60 min
Transfer pressure dip (>2s)    | No                    | Stop fill; maintenance assessment; deviation         | QA immediate; Supervisor
Pump vibration anomaly          | No                    | Stop fill; maintenance check                         | Supervisor immediate
Temperature room out-of-range  | No                    | Environmental alert; HVAC check                     | QA + Facilities
EM particle count exceedance   | No                    | Immediate QA notification; line assessment           | QA immediate
MES process alarm (general)    | Yes                   | Acknowledge + document reason + monitor              | Supervisor if recurs same type >3/shift
Fill speed deviation            | Yes                   | Acknowledge + adjust + document                     | Supervisor if not resolved in 10 min
Clear-Clean-Check open at RUN  | SYSTEM SHOULD PREVENT | Deviation; HOLD; QA notification                    | QA immediately (not yet enforced by MES; human obligation)

24.3 Key Principle (Restated)
Alarm acknowledgement is not corrective action. It is a notification that a condition was observed. The required action is what constitutes a response. An alarm acknowledged but not acted upon and not documented is a deviation.

24.4 Alarm Trend Review Obligation
Shift supervisor must review alarm count at shift midpoint and shift end.
If alarm count for any single type exceeds 3 per shift: mandatory deviation note, even if each individual event was auto-recovered.
Alarm review results must be documented in manufacturing logbook shift summary.

25. MEASUREMENT AND TESTING OBLIGATIONS AT RESTART
25.1 Why This Section Was Added
Section 12 of MFG-LOG-CF882-B202 shows all in-process measurement during Batch CF-882 was at standard frequency designed for normal fill-finish operations. Post-maintenance restart events represent an elevated contamination risk window requiring additional measurement to detect potential process-contact particulate contributions.

25.2 Additional Measurement Requirements After Product-Contact Maintenance
The following additional measurements are mandatory for the first 2 hours after any product-contact maintenance restart:

Measurement M-1: Enhanced visual inspection.
  - First 200 vials filled after restart: 100 percent manual visual inspection at controlled booth speed.
  - If defective vials detected (any foreign particulate): immediate HOLD; quarantine all post-restart vials; QA notification.
  - If no defects in first 200: continue enhanced sampling at 1-in-20 (5 percent) until 2-hour window complete.
  - Document enhanced inspection results in batch record.

Measurement M-2: In-process sub-visible particle spot test.
  - Collect 5 filled vials at 15 minutes after restart.
  - QC to perform rapid sub-visible particle count (USP <788>-equivalent method).
  - Alert limit: >25 particles ≥10 µm per mL.
  - If alert exceeded: HOLD; full investigation; do not continue fill.
  - This is a triggered in-process test, not a standard release test.

Measurement M-3: Transfer pressure trend confirmation.
  - MES operator to actively monitor pressure trend for first 30 minutes; document readings every 5 minutes manually.
  - Any reading outside normal range: alarm response per Section 24 alarm matrix.

25.3 Post-Restart Report (new requirement)
A Post-Restart Quality Report must be completed within 1 hour of the 2-hour enhanced monitoring window:
  - Summarize enhanced visual results.
  - Summarize sub-visible particle spot-test result.
  - Confirm no alarm escalation events.
  - QA Witness to sign Post-Restart Quality Report.
  - If report cannot be completed or any finding is out-of-specification: HOLD pending QA disposition.
  - Post-Restart Quality Report filed with batch packet.

26. CHANGE CONTROL OBLIGATIONS FOR LINE EQUIPMENT
26.1 What Requires Change Control on FL-02
The following changes to FL-02 equipment or its supporting systems require formal change control under POL-QA-028 regardless of whether they are described as maintenance:
  - Any repositioning of hose routing, supporting clips, or brackets.
  - Replacement of transfer hose with a different material, diameter, or length.
  - Modification to pump mounting, alignment, or support structure.
  - Change to nozzle type, manifold configuration, or fill path geometry.
  - Software update to MES terminal that affects process parameter monitoring or documented controls.
  - Addition or removal of any in-line sensor.
  - Change to EM sampling point locations.
  - Any upgrade or substitution within the product-contact wetted path.

26.2 Like-For-Like Maintenance Scope Clarification
"Like-for-like" replacement means: identical part number, identical material, identical configuration, identical position. If any of these four criteria is not met, change control is required.
The historic misclassification of hose routing changes as like-for-like is explicitly corrected by this section.

26.3 Change Control Training Obligation
All maintenance technicians assigned to FL-02 must complete the "Change Control for Maintenance Personnel" module (TRN-MOD-MNT-CC-01) before performing any equipment maintenance on product-contact components. This module was added to the mandatory maintenance qualification program following INV-2026-0019.
