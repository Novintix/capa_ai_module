INTRODUCTION
This compendium consolidates operational policies applicable to deviation management, data integrity, line clearance, alarm handling, shift handover, documentation practice, CAPA, training, change control, and investigation governance. It is intended as a single-source policy reference for internal audit, investigation support, and mock-data simulation activities.

POLICY 1 - DEVIATION MANAGEMENT AND ESCALATION
Policy ID: POL-QA-005
Purpose:
- Define expectations for identification, recording, escalation, investigation, and closure of deviations.
Requirements:
1. Any departure from approved procedure or specification is a deviation.
2. Deviations must be recorded in real time.
3. Product-contact equipment interventions require immediate QA notification when restart controls are incomplete.
4. A deviation cannot be closed without documented root cause and impact assessment.
5. Major and Critical deviations require CAPA initiation within 5 business days.
6. Repeat deviations trigger effectiveness review and management escalation.

POLICY 2 - GOOD DOCUMENTATION PRACTICE (GDP)
Policy ID: POL-QA-012
Purpose:
- Ensure records are attributable, legible, contemporaneous, original, accurate, complete, consistent, enduring, and available.
Requirements:
1. Entries must be made at time of activity.
2. Backdating is prohibited.
3. Corrections require single-line strike-through, reason, date, and signature.
4. Blank mandatory fields are non-compliant records.
5. Verbal claims without record support are not acceptable evidence.

POLICY 3 - DATA INTEGRITY GOVERNANCE
Policy ID: POL-QA-017
Purpose:
- Define controls preventing data manipulation, omission, unauthorized changes, or undocumented reconstruction.
Requirements:
1. Audit trails must remain enabled for electronic systems.
2. Shared credentials are prohibited.
3. Any delayed entry requires documented justification and supervisory approval.
4. Data review must include exception and trend checks.
5. Reconstructed records are classified as data integrity events unless justified and approved.

POLICY 4 - LINE CLEARANCE AND POST-MAINTENANCE RESTART
Policy ID: POL-MFG-044
Purpose:
- Ensure safe and compliant restart after interventions affecting product-contact pathways.
Requirements:
1. No restart before Clear-Clean-Check completion.
2. Witness signature required prior to line release.
3. Missing witness signature invalidates line clearance record.
4. Open clearance item at shift boundary requires written handover and line hold unless QA waiver granted.
5. Any restart without complete clearance triggers deviation and risk assessment.

POLICY 5 - ALARM MANAGEMENT AND RESPONSE
Policy ID: POL-ENG-031
Purpose:
- Standardize handling of equipment and process alarms.
Requirements:
1. Alarm acknowledgment is not corrective action.
2. Recurring alarm without root action requires escalation.
3. Critical alarm linked to product-contact operations requires immediate hold pending assessment.
4. Alarm logs must be reconciled with batch chronology during review.

POLICY 6 - SHIFT HANDOVER GOVERNANCE
Policy ID: POL-MFG-031
Purpose:
- Ensure continuity and ownership across shifts.
Requirements:
1. Open quality, maintenance, or safety items must be transferred in signed written format.
2. Verbal handover alone is prohibited for open compliance items.
3. Incoming shift must acknowledge acceptance of each open item.
4. Unassigned open item at handover is classified as control failure.

POLICY 7 - INVESTIGATION AND ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS
Policy ID: POL-QA-021
Purpose:
- Govern investigation quality and root cause standards.
Requirements:
1. Investigations must include timeline, evidence matrix, and cause-contribution analysis.
2. Hypotheses must be evidence-based and testable.
3. Conclusions require corroboration from at least two independent evidence sources when feasible.
4. Interim conclusions must be clearly marked and updated with final disposition.

POLICY 8 - CAPA GOVERNANCE
Policy ID: POL-QA-022
Purpose:
- Ensure corrective and preventive actions are timely, measurable, and effective.
Requirements:
1. CAPA shall include root cause, action owner, due date, and objective effectiveness criteria.
2. Effectiveness checks must use measurable outcomes, not only activity completion.
3. Delayed CAPA requires documented risk acceptance by Quality leadership.
4. Repeat issue within 12 months triggers CAPA redesign review.

POLICY 9 - TRAINING AND QUALIFICATION
Policy ID: POL-HR-QMS-014
Purpose:
- Ensure personnel competence for GMP-critical tasks.
Requirements:
1. Task qualification required before independent execution.
2. Annual refresher required for SOPs tagged high criticality.
3. Training effectiveness must be monitored using deviations, audit findings, and observation trends.
4. Shift supervisors must verify active qualification status before assignment.

POLICY 10 - CHANGE CONTROL
Policy ID: POL-QA-028
Purpose:
- Govern controlled implementation of process, procedure, and system changes.
Requirements:
1. Changes affecting product-contact flow require formal impact assessment.
2. Temporary changes need expiry and rollback controls.
3. Related SOP and form revisions must be synchronized.
4. Training must be completed before change effective date.

POLICY 11 - BATCH RECORD REVIEW AND RELEASE SUPPORT
Policy ID: POL-QA-034
Purpose:
- Define minimum checks for record completeness supporting release decisions.
Requirements:
1. Mandatory checklist: signatures, timestamps, interventions, holds, alarms, deviations.
2. Missing critical records prevent release recommendation.
3. Late entries are reviewed for potential data integrity risk.

POLICY 12 - RISK MANAGEMENT AND ESCALATION
Policy ID: POL-QA-040
Purpose:
- Integrate risk principles into operational decisions.
Requirements:
1. Potential patient impact events must be escalated immediately.
2. Risk scoring considers severity, occurrence likelihood, and detectability.
3. Interim controls must be documented and tracked to closure.

POLICY 13 - MANAGEMENT REVIEW GOVERNANCE
Policy ID: POL-QA-050
Purpose:
- Define requirements for effective periodic management review of the quality management system.
Requirements:
1. Monthly Manufacturing Quality Review must include standing agenda items for CAPA status, deviation trends, and open commitments.
2. Open CAPA items must be tiered by residual risk level: Low, Medium, High.
3. Medium residual risk CAPAs exceeding target closure date by more than 30 days require documented risk acceptance by QA Director.
4. High residual risk CAPAs exceeding target closure date require immediate Site Director notification and formal risk acceptance within 5 business days.
5. "In progress" status notation without risk assessment is not acceptable for items older than 60 days past original due date.
6. Management review minutes must record decisions made, not only status updates.
7. Any action item that was deprioritized from its original scope or timeline must be noted with risk impact assessment.
8. Annual substantive review of QMS processes must be supplemented by event-triggered reviews when a critical process failure occurs within a previously reviewed area.
Note on Policy 13 current status:

- Management Review template last revised 2023; criteria for risk-tier differentiation not present.
- This gap contributed to failure to escalate ITR-2024-0443 delay in 2025 management reviews.
- POL-QA-050 is a proposed revision arising from INV-2026-0019; current version (effective date 2025-01-01) does not yet contain requirements 2-8 above.
- Required revision target: 2026-Q1.

POLICY 14 - IT SYSTEM PROJECT GOVERNANCE FOR QUALITY-CRITICAL CONTROLS
Policy ID: POL-IT-QMS-015
Purpose:
- Ensure that IT project decisions affecting quality-critical system controls are subject to QA oversight and formal risk documentation.
Requirements:
1. Any IT project containing a quality system control action (e.g., system lock, audit trail, electronic signature, validation) must be registered in the Quality-Critical IT Project Register.
2. Priority changes, timeline extensions, or scope reductions for Quality-Critical IT projects require mandatory QA review and written approval before the change takes effect.
3. If a Quality-Critical IT project is delayed beyond its original target date, a formal risk acceptance document must be generated within 15 business days of identified delay.
4. Risk acceptance for delayed Quality-Critical IT projects must be signed by QA Director.
5. Compensating controls must be documented and placed under active monitoring for the duration of any gap period.
6. QA reviews Quality-Critical IT project status monthly at the Management Review; delays are escalated per POL-QA-050.
Note on Policy 14 current status:
- No formal Quality-Critical IT Project Register existed at time of CF-882 event.
- IT project prioritization process (managed by Operations/IT joint committee) had no mandatory QA approval step.
- ITR-2024-0443 was downgraded in priority in Q2 2025 without QA notification.
- This structural gap is identified as a root contributing factor in Why Level 7 of INV-2026-0019.
- POL-IT-QMS-015 is a proposed new policy arising from INV-2026-0019.

POLICY 15 - PRODUCTION PERFORMANCE KPI GOVERNANCE AND COMPLIANCE ALIGNMENT
Policy ID: POL-OPS-QMS-022
Purpose:
- Ensure that production performance metrics do not create implicit incentive structures that discourage or penalize correct compliance behavior.
Requirements:
1. Shift performance scorecards must include compliance adherence as a minimum 25 percent weighting in the overall score.
2. Compliance-mandated line holds, where the hold decision is confirmed as correct by QA, shall be recorded as a positive compliance action and shall not reduce throughput score proportionately.
3. Production KPIs must be reviewed by Site Quality Council annually for potential compliance-inhibiting effects.
4. Any KPI design change must include a formal assessment of compliance behavior impact before implementation.
5. Production Director is jointly accountable with QA Director for compliance adherence KPI outcomes.
6. Supervisors who initiate a compliance-mandated hold correctly and promptly are recognized in formal shift feedback.
7. Failure to initiate a compliance-mandated hold when required is reflected in individual performance review.
Note on Policy 15 current status:
- Current production scorecard is 100 percent throughput-based. No compliance adherence weighting.
- Site committed to KPI revision following Obs-2024-INS-07.
- Revised KPI framework in draft as of 2025-Q4; not yet approved or implemented.
- Throughput-only KPI was a directly-cited contributing factor in supervisor behavior analysis for INV-2026-0019.
- POL-OPS-QMS-022 proposed as formal policy to replace informal direction; target effective date 2026-Q2.

POLICY 16 - CAPA EFFECTIVENESS VERIFICATION STANDARDS
Policy ID: POL-QA-022-EFF
Purpose:
- Supplement POL-QA-022 with minimum standards for what constitutes a valid CAPA effectiveness assessment.
Requirements:
1. CAPA effectiveness criteria must be defined at CAPA opening, not at closure review.
2. For CAPAs involving technical controls (system locks, automated checks, IT-dependent features): effectiveness is not confirmed until control deployment is verified by a system test or QA audit observation.
3. Activity completion (training done, SOP revised) is a necessary but not sufficient condition for effectiveness confirmation.
4. Effectiveness reports must explicitly state: (a) what was verified, (b) the method of verification, (c) whether any underlying technical control has been deployed and confirmed.
5. Residual risk notation requires accompanying risk tier assignment and, if Medium or High, formal risk acceptance documentation.
6. Effectiveness reviews for CAPAs with "residual risk - medium" or higher must include a timeline for when residual risk is expected to be resolved and who is accountable.
7. If a CAPA closed as effective is followed by a recurrence within 18 months, the prior effectiveness assessment is retrospectively reviewed for methodology gaps.
Note on Policy 16 current status:
- Current CAPA effectiveness review template (form FRM-QA-CAPA-EFF-01, version 2022) contains activity completion checkboxes only.
- No field requiring technical control deployment confirmation.
- No requirement for risk-tier assignment for residual risk notation.
- CAPA-2024-0097-EFF used this template and was declared effective without verifying MES lock deployment.
- Template revision (FRM-QA-CAPA-EFF-01) planned as part of CAPA from INV-2026-0019.

POLICY 17 - WITNESS AVAILABILITY AND PRE-BOOKING FOR POST-MAINTENANCE RESTARTS
Policy ID: POL-QA-MFG-WIT-001
Purpose:
- Prevent QA witness availability gaps that create conditions for restart control failure.
Requirements:
1. All planned post-maintenance interventions of type "Product-Contact Intervention" must have a QA witness pre-assigned at least 2 hours before planned restart window.
2. QA resource planning for each shift must account for all open work orders involving planned product-contact maintenance on that shift.
3. If QA witness is not available within 30 minutes of planned restart, shift supervisor must formally delay restart and notify QA Manager.
4. QA Duty Manager is available on-call for witness-related escalations during all production shifts.
5. Ad-hoc (unplanned) maintenance interventions on product-contact equipment must prompt immediate QA notification and witness assignment before any restart.
6. QA witness availability record must appear in the shift planning log; gap in planning is a control deficiency.
Note on Policy 17 current status:
- No formal QA witness pre-booking process existed at time of CF-882 event.
- QA floor monitor was assigned to another critical review at time of planned CF-882 restart.
- Temporary control (pre-booking) introduced as immediate action following DEV-2026-0019.
- POL-QA-MFG-WIT-001 is a proposed formal policy from INV-2026-0019 remediation.

POLICY 18 - MATERIALS AND SUPPLIER QUALITY MANAGEMENT
Policy ID: POL-QA-MAT-018
Purpose:
- Ensure all materials used in manufacturing are of confirmed quality and fully traceable from receipt through use.
Requirements:
1. All incoming materials must have a Certificate of Analysis (CoA) confirming specification compliance.
2. Incoming inspection must be completed and approved before any material is released to manufacturing.
3. Material traceability must be maintained from supplier lot to finished batch: supplier name, CoA reference, and incoming inspection report captured for every material used.
4. Critical materials in product-contact pathways (seals, hoses, gaskets) must have a particle release risk assessment documented in their material qualification record.
5. No like-for-like maintenance exemption applies to materials whose geometry, routing, or physical configuration affects particulate shed risk potential.
6. Supplier change for any product-contact component requires formal change control and re-qualification.
7. Material disposition records retained for 5 years or product shelf life + 1 year, whichever longer.
Policy gap identified in INV-2026-0019:
- Transfer hose exterior abrasion at routing clip zone was not part of any periodic material integrity review.
- Hose routing change was not evaluated for particulate risk because no re-assessment was triggered.
- POL-QA-MAT-018 Requirement 4 and 5 are new requirements arising from INV-2026-0019.

POLICY 19 - CONTROLLED ENVIRONMENT AND CLEANROOM MANAGEMENT
Policy ID: POL-ENV-019
Purpose:
- Define requirements for the manufacturing environment to prevent contamination from facility, HVAC, and cleanroom management failures.
Requirements:
1. Cleanroom EM must follow a site-validated sampling plan with defined alert and action limits for viable and non-viable contamination.
2. EM exceedances require immediate investigation, containment, and documented CAPA.
3. HVAC systems serving product-contact areas must be on a validated preventive maintenance schedule; no PM may lapse by more than 14 days.
4. Differential pressure between cleanroom grades must be continuously monitored and alarmed; pressure exceedance is an immediate deviation trigger.
5. Temperature and humidity must remain within validated ranges throughout production; any exceedance must be documented and assessed for product impact.
6. EM is a supporting negative control: within-specification EM confirms room-level environmental control but does not substitute for in-process product-contact contamination monitoring.
7. Areas where product-contact maintenance events occurred must have enhanced EM sampling for the next manufacturing campaign.
Environment status for CF-882: All EM parameters within specification. Environmental contamination confirmed as eliminated cause.

POLICY 20 - IN-PROCESS MEASUREMENT AND MONITORING
Policy ID: POL-QC-IPC-020
Purpose:
- Define in-process measurement and monitoring requirements providing real-time product quality assurance.
Requirements:
1. Each production line must have a documented In-Process Control (IPC) plan specifying measurement type, frequency, acceptance limits, and response actions.
2. IPC plans reviewed by QA and QC; updated annually and after any process or equipment change.
3. Supplemental IPC must be activated for elevated-risk scenarios including post product-contact maintenance restart.
4. Supplemental IPC for post-maintenance restart must include: enhanced visual inspection (100 percent for first 200 units), sub-visible particle spot-test, and extended process parameter trending.
5. In-process measurement results must be recorded contemporaneously.
6. IPC action-limit exceedance requires immediate HOLD and QA notification.
7. IPC sampling must have a documented statistical basis; arbitrary reduced sampling without documented justification is a deviation.
IPC gap identified in INV-2026-0019:
- No supplemental IPC plan existed for post-maintenance restart.
- Standard IPC was applied throughout Batch CF-882; elevated risk period was not addressed with heightened measurement.
- Requirements 3 and 4 are new, arising from INV-2026-0019.

POLICY 21 - EQUIPMENT QUALIFICATION AND CHANGE CONTROL FOR PRODUCT-CONTACT SYSTEMS
Policy ID: POL-ENG-QUA-021
Purpose:
- Ensure product-contact equipment is qualified, change-controlled, and maintained in a validated state throughout operational life.
Requirements:
1. All product-contact equipment subject to IQ/OQ/PQ before first use.
2. Post-maintenance verification (PMV) required after any product-contact intervention before return to production service.
3. PMV failure constitutes equipment hold; production cannot commence until PMV passes.
4. Any change to product-contact equipment geometry, material, routing, or support configuration requires formal change control regardless of whether described as maintenance.
5. Like-for-like maintenance classification must be documented; it does not apply where position, routing, or load conditions are changed even if parts are identical.
6. Equipment qualification plans must include a particulate-risk section evaluating shed potential for all wetted-path materials.
7. Calibration schedules must specify accuracy requirements and criticality level; product-contact equipment sensors calibrated at frequency matching process parameter criticality.
8. Equipment qualification status confirmed before batch start; batch must not start where any critical equipment qualification has lapsed.
Equipment qualification gap identified in INV-2026-0019:
- Transfer hose routing changed without change control; no particulate risk re-assessment performed.
- PMV for P-17 pump-head replacement not completed before filling commenced.
- Hose exterior inspection criteria not in FRM-MFG-022 or any qualification standard.

POLICY 22 - HUMAN FACTORS AND ERGONOMIC RISK MANAGEMENT
Policy ID: POL-HR-HF-022
Purpose:
- Integrate human factors and ergonomic risk principles into manufacturing process design, shift planning, and compliance system design.
Requirements:
1. Shift scheduling must guarantee minimum 8-hour rest intervals for personnel in safety-critical roles; exceptions require documented risk assessment and supervisory approval.
2. Critical compliance decision points (restart authority, deviation initiation, hold enforcement) must be supported by minimized concurrent tasks and distraction.
3. Documentation stations for critical control forms must be co-located with or digitally integrated into operator workstations to minimize physical friction of real-time documentation.
4. Communications creating production urgency signals at critical shift decision windows must be reviewed by QA in incident investigations to assess whether they contributed to compliance decision errors.
5. Senior management communications about production urgency must include a compliance-first reminder when issued within 24 hours of a planned post-maintenance restart or high-compliance-risk operation.
6. Shift workload assessment must be performed for batches combining maintenance restart events with schedule-recovery pressure.
7. Mentor selection for new employee onboarding must include a behavioral compliance assessment; mentors with documented deferred-documentation patterns must complete remedial behavioral training before mentoring duties are assigned.
Human factors gaps identified in INV-2026-0019:
- J. Thomas had insufficient rest interval. Document station spatially separated from fill station. Planning urgency email sent the day before a high-risk batch with no compliance qualifier. Mentor transmitted deferred-documentation behavioral norm to mentee without assessment or mitigation.

APPENDIX B - ENFORCEMENT PRINCIPLES
- Non-compliance severity determines immediate containment requirements.
- Repeat findings are treated as systemic risk indicators.
- Policy exceptions require formal approval and documented rationale.
- Policy exception approvals must reference the specific risk accepted and include a time-bound resolution plan.
- Verbal policy exception approvals are not permitted; all exceptions must be documented with time, date, approver identification, and scope of exception.
- Repeat policy exceptions in the same area within 12 months trigger mandatory QA Manager review.
- Policies are living documents; INV-2026-0019 identified 5 new policies (18-22) and 3 material revisions to existing policies. All listed with their INV-2026-0019 reference for traceability.
