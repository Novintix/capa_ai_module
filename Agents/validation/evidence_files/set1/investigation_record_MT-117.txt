INVESTIGATION RECORD
Investigation Number: INV-MDT-2026-0047

Investigation Type: Product quality event with process governance and documentation control concerns
Initiation Date: 2026-03-03
Investigation Owner: QA Investigation Cell - MedTech Division

1. INCIDENT DESCRIPTION
During final outgoing inspection, 186 cartridge sets out of 12,000 were rejected for visible translucent fiber-like particulate in the fluid pathway chamber. The reject ratio exceeded internal action limits and triggered formal investigation.

2. INVESTIGATION OBJECTIVES
- Determine direct and contributing causes.
- Assess patient and clinical-use risk.
- Verify adherence to maintenance-restart controls and handover governance.
- Define immediate containment and long-term CAPA recommendations.

3. SCOPE
- Manufacturing records for 2026-02-12 batch run IPC-117A.
- Maintenance and restart controls related to ultrasonic weld horn replacement.
- Shift handover records and policy compliance.
- Process trend data, particulate analysis, and historical CAPA records.

4. DOCUMENTS REVIEWED
- ASSY-LOG-IPC117A-MT117 (assembly and shift logbook)
- SOP-MDT-058 (post-maintenance line clearance and restart control)
- policy.txt compendium (site governance policies)
- assembly_speed_env_MT-117-process_data.csv (time-series process data)
- stability_report_MT-117.txt (quality and reliability report)
- historical_capa_medtech.txt (prior trend and recurrence records)
- employee_training_records_medtech.txt (qualification and competency records)

5. EVIDENCE MATRIX
E1: Restart occurred while Line Readiness Form FRM-MDT-041 witness field remained blank.
E2: Open compliance item was transferred verbally during shift handover.
E3: No immediate deviation was raised when trigger condition was met.
E4: Throughput recovery pressure documented in timeline and planning communication.
E5: Historical CAPA data shows repeated weakness in handover and real-time documentation.
E6: Particulate morphology is consistent with polymer shed from worn guide rail sleeve near tubing feed path.

8.1 Primary Cause

8.2 Contributing Cause 1

8.3 Contributing Cause 2
- Throughput pressure influenced decision-making.
8.4 Contributing Cause 3

8.5 Contributing Cause 4

9. RISK ASSESSMENT
Severity: High (sterile fluid pathway particulate risk)
Occurrence: Medium (prior signals in CAPA trend)
Detectability: Medium (detected at final stage, not prevented at source)
Initial Risk Level: High

10. CONTAINMENT ACTIONS
- Quarantine all 12,000 units from IPC-117A.
- Expand visual and sub-visible particulate reinspection scope.
- Temporary rule: QA pre-assigned for all post-maintenance restarts.
- Issue controlled shift briefing on written handover requirement.
- Replace and inspect tubing feed guide rail sleeves on line AS-04.

11. PROPOSED CAPA PLAN
CAPA-1: Deploy MES lock to block RUN transition while FRM-MDT-041 witness field is blank.
CAPA-2: Auto-prompt deviation ticket creation when restart occurs before checklist closure.
CAPA-3: Add mandatory QA approval in IT priority changes for quality-critical controls.
CAPA-4: Revise supervisor KPIs to include compliance-hold positive weighting.
CAPA-5: Implement scenario-based retraining for escalation under schedule pressure.
CAPA-6: Add maintenance work order field confirming readiness form initiation before area handoff.
CAPA-7: Add periodic particulate-shed inspection to guide rail sleeve PM checklist.

12. EFFECTIVENESS CRITERIA
- 100 percent post-maintenance restarts with completed witness sign-off before RUN.
- Zero verbal-only transfer of open critical items for 90 days.
- At least 90 percent reduction in delayed critical entries within two quarters.
- No particulate reject trend above alert limit for three consecutive production months.

14. SYSTEMIC WHY ANALYSIS (7 LEVELS)
WHY 1: Why were defective units produced?
Because polymer-like fibers entered the sterile fluid pathway during cartridge assembly.

WHY 2: Why did fibers enter the pathway?
Because line restart occurred without complete post-maintenance readiness verification, and material-shed risk near tubing path was not detected before operation resumed.

WHY 3: Why was readiness verification incomplete at restart?
Because RUN status was allowed while FRM-MDT-041 witness field remained blank.

WHY 4: Why was RUN allowed and not stopped immediately?
Because MES had no witness-field lock and supervisor chose continuation under throughput pressure.

WHY 5: Why was MES lock absent and supervision weak?
Because quality-critical IT control deployment was delayed without formal risk acceptance, while KPI design penalized holds and rewarded throughput.

WHY 6: Why were these delays and KPI gaps tolerated?
Because CAPA effectiveness checks emphasized activity completion over technical control verification, and governance lacked formal trigger rules for overdue high-risk actions.

WHY 7: Why did governance lack these trigger rules?
Because management review and IT prioritization procedures were not updated after prior observations, leaving no mandatory risk-tier escalation pathway for deferred quality-critical controls.

15. ORGANIZATIONAL FACTOR ANALYSIS
15.1 Culture of Deferred Closure
Operators and supervisors described an informal norm: close documentation at the next convenient window rather than before restart.

15.2 Authority Gradient
Operators deferred stop authority to QA rather than exercising line-hold responsibility themselves.

15.3 KPI and Behavioral Incentives
Throughput-only performance metrics created implicit penalty for compliance-related holds.

15.4 CAPA Confidence Without Outcome Verification
Past CAPA closures signaled completion but did not confirm deployment of core technical controls.

17. ISHIKAWA 6M SUMMARY
MAN: Delayed documentation and non-escalation under pressure.
MACHINE: No MES lock; worn guide rail sleeve near tubing feed path.
METHOD: Incomplete adherence to restart and handover procedures.
MEASUREMENT: Detection occurred late at final inspection rather than in-process control.
MATERIAL: Polymer fiber profile linked to guide rail sleeve abrasion.
ENVIRONMENT: No cleanroom excursion signal; environmental trend not primary driver.

18. FINAL INVESTIGATION CONCLUSION
The IPC-117A event is primarily a governance and control-enforcement failure in post-maintenance restart and handover management, compounded by undeployed technical controls and throughput-biased incentives. Particulate characterization supports a process-contact-adjacent polymer shed source. Investigation recommends immediate containment, systemic CAPA, and governance redesign focused on enforceable controls.
