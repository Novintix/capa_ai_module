PERFORMANCE AND QUALITY ASSESSMENT REPORT
Report Number: PQR-2026-0173
Product: NeuroPulse Implantable Infusion Controller (Model NP-410)
Lot: MD-317
Legacy Evidence Tag: D-317
Issued By: Quality Control and Reliability Lab
Issue Date: 2026-03-02

1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Lot MD-317 showed elevated low-dose flow-rate failures and increased micro-particulate indications in optical inspection. Core electronic performance remained within specification. Combined evidence indicates strongest linkage to process governance failure during post-maintenance restart, with humidity drift as an amplifying environmental factor.

2. PURPOSE
Consolidate acceptance test outcomes, particulate characterization, process correlation, and risk interpretation for formal investigation and CAPA decisions.

3. SAMPLE PLAN AND METHODS
3.1 Device sampling
- Outgoing acceptance testing performed per NP410-QC-ATP-09.
- Additional retain sample testing selected from early, middle, and late assembly windows.

3.2 Flow performance testing
- Low-dose profile at 0.8 mL/hr, 2-hour controlled bench run.
- Mid-dose and high-dose profiles used for comparative stability checks.

3.3 Particulate screening
- Optical path inspection at inline camera and post-assembly microscopy.
- Selected suspect samples analyzed by SEM-EDS.

3.4 Ancillary review
- Chronology and control-state correlation from assembly log and process CSV.

4. RESULTS
4.1 Flow-rate verification
- Failed units at low-dose profile: 19/1,200 (1.58 percent).
- Site action limit: 0.80 percent.
- Alert limit: 0.50 percent.
- Mid-dose and high-dose profiles: within expected control limits.

4.2 Particulate findings
- Inline optical suspect events rose during 09:00-16:00 window.
- Final microscopy-confirmed suspect units: 14.
- Predominant morphology: thin polymer-like filaments.

4.3 Electronics and firmware checks
- Pump motor current, encoder response, and firmware checksum all in-spec.
- No systemic electronic fault pattern found.

4.4 Comparative lot trend
- Prior three lots on MDA-02 did not exceed low-dose failure alert limit.
- MD-317 exceeds historical mean by 4.2x.

5. CORRELATION WITH PROCESS EVIDENCE
- Restart occurred before sterile-path witness completion.
- Open critical item transferred verbally at shift boundary.
- Deviation raised only after extended delay.
- Relative humidity was elevated during the same period as particulate and low-dose instability rise.

6. PARTICULATE CHARACTERIZATION SUMMARY
Method: SEM-EDS with supplemental FTIR
Sample source: 6 retained devices with confirmed suspect material
Analysis date: 2026-03-01
External lab reference: EXT-MDT-260301

Finding 1:
- Morphology: filament fragments, 90-260 micrometers length.
- EDS: carbon/oxygen dominant with trace silicon.
- FTIR match: polyurethane-like profile, likely from tubing sheath or polymer handling surface.

Finding 2:
- Secondary fragments: short opaque particles 20-60 micrometers.
- Composition: mixed polymer-cellulosic signature.
- Interpretation: likely ancillary handling lint, not primary source.

Material comparison:
- Transfer tubing abrasion sample showed strong profile overlap with primary particles.
- Weld horn residue sample did not match.
- Gowning fibers were broader diameter than detected fragments.

Preliminary source attribution:
- Most likely source: polymer shedding at transfer tubing clip abrasion point during post-maintenance handling.
- Confidence: moderate-high.

7. RISK INTERPRETATION
Quality Risk: High
Compliance Risk: High
Patient Risk: Medium-High pending final disposition due implantable use context and low-dose delivery impact potential.

8. RECOMMENDATIONS
Immediate
1. Maintain lot quarantine and perform 100 percent low-dose retest.
2. Execute enhanced particulate inspection on all quarantined units.
3. Enforce witness-first restart rule with no exceptions.

Near-term
1. Deploy MES lock for restart gate.
2. Replace and reroute tubing clip geometry to eliminate abrasion mechanism.
3. Revise KPI model to remove throughput penalty for compliance holds.
4. Introduce scenario-based supervisor escalation drills.

9. LIMITATIONS
- Final attribution confidence depends on full teardown review of all suspect units.
- Some chronology evidence relies on manual log entries and requires full audit-trail reconciliation.

10. CONCLUSION
The lot MD-317 event is most consistent with a process-control and governance failure at post-maintenance restart, amplified by environmental and material-handling factors. Electronic subsystem integrity was not identified as a primary failure driver.
