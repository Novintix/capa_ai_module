LESSONS LEARNED RELEVANT TO CURRENT INVESTIGATION
1. Restart controls fail most often when maintenance completion occurs near throughput pressure windows.
2. Signed written handover is the strongest barrier against open-item loss.
3. Documentation backfill intent is a leading indicator of control failure.
4. Training completion alone does not guarantee behavioral compliance.

CAPA RECORD 5
CAPA ID: CAPA-2025-0038-EFF
Date Opened: 2025-08-01
Date Closed: 2025-10-30
Area: Effectiveness review of CAPA-2025-0038 (data integrity and delayed recording)
Background:
- CAPA-2025-0038 introduced MES real-time checkpoint prompts and mid-shift documentation audits.
- Effectiveness review initiated at 3-month mark.
Effectiveness Findings:
- Delayed entries reduced by 62 percent as reported; however, reduction was concentrated in non-critical documentation.
- Critical control records (line clearance, restart verification) showed only 38 percent reduction in delayed entries.
- Root of residual gap: MES checkpoint prompts were silenced by operators in high-throughput periods; system allowed mute without generating alert.
Actions from Effectiveness Review:
- Checkpoint muting now generates supervisory alert.
- Critical record categories separated from general documentation in delay tracking.
- Escalation added: two delayed critical records in one shift generates automatic QA notification.
Residual Risk:
- Behavioral tendency to defer documentation under schedule pressure remains; system prompts alone are insufficient without cultural reinforcement.
Lessons Learned:
- System-level prompts without enforcement teeth carry limited effectiveness.
- Behavioral change requires management signaling that throughput is not valued over compliance.
- This lesson was not yet translated into KPI reform at the time this CAPA closed.

CAPA RECORD 7
CAPA ID: CAPA-2025-0189
Date Opened: 2025-12-10
Date Closed: Open
Area: KPI governance misalignment - throughput pressure contributing to compliance failures
Background:
- Opened following 2024 regulatory inspection observation Obs-2024-INS-07 and subsequent internal trend analysis.
- Observer noted implicit pressure created by production throughput KPIs that do not include compliance behavior weighting.
Investigation Findings:
- Current shift performance score is 100 percent throughput-based (vials produced vs. planned).
- Compliance-related line holds reduce shift score regardless of whether the hold was a correct compliance action.
- Supervisors reported informally that line holds for documentation reasons generate negative comments in daily operations reviews.
- Three separate interviews in CAPA-2024-0097 and CAPA-2025-0038 cited "fear of negative performance impact" when asked about delay in compliance stops.
Proposed Actions:
- Revise shift performance scorecard to include 30 percent compliance adherence weighting.
- Add specific positive credit for compliance-mandated holds executed correctly and timely.
- Remove throughput penalty for holds where root cause is confirmed as compliance-mandated.
- Implement communication campaign from site leadership reinforcing compliance-first culture.
Actions Status:
- Scorecard revision: draft submitted to Production Director and HR; pending approval.
- Leadership communication: Site Director issued memo November 2025; QA assessing whether behavioral impact is measurable.
- Formal KPI rollout: planned 2026-Q1.
Connection to batch CF-882 event:
- KPI misalignment is identified as a deep systemic contributing factor in INV-2026-0019.
- This CAPA represents the organizational-level corrective action for the throughput-pressure layer of the causal chain.

CAPA RECORD 8
CAPA ID: CAPA-2024-0097-EFF
Date Opened: 2024-12-06
Date Closed: 2025-02-28
Area: Effectiveness review for CAPA-2024-0097 (restart governance and handover)
Background:
- CAPA-2024-0097 added the no-restart clause to SOP-MFG-044 and required e-batch packet checklist validation.
- Effectiveness check planned 3 months post-closure.
Effectiveness Results:
- 11 maintenance restarts audited in the 3-month window.
- 10 restarts: complete paperwork before RUN status. Compliant.
- 1 restart: delayed entry of witness sign-off; documented as minor deviation.
- Effectiveness declaration: "Effective with residual risk."
Critical Gap in Effectiveness Assessment:
- The effectiveness check verified whether operators completed paperwork, but did not verify whether the MES system lock (ITR-2024-0443) had been deployed as the intended technical control.
- Effectiveness checklist used activity completion (SOP revised, training done, 10/11 audits passed) rather than system control verification as primary criteria.
- The "residual risk" notation was documented but did not trigger re-escalation or risk acceptance documentation.
- No formal risk acceptance was signed by QA leadership for the period between CAPA-2024-0097 closure and MES lock deployment.
What This Means:
- CAPA-2024-0097 was closed while the core technical control (MES lock) was unconfirmed deployed.
- This created a false assurance that the risk was adequately controlled.
- 11 months later, when batch CF-882 event occurred, the same gap that CAPA-2024-0097 was designed to prevent was still present.
Lesson:
- Effectiveness criteria must explicitly confirm technical control deployment, not infer it from activity completion.
- Management review should have caught this gap during the 11-month window between closure and recurrence.

SYSTEMIC PATTERN ANALYSIS (UPDATED THROUGH 2025)
Pattern A: Technical control deployment lag
- MES lock identified as required control in CAPA-2024-0097 (closure Dec 2024).
- MES lock still undeployed at batch CF-882 event (Nov 2025): 11-month gap.
- Root: IT project prioritization; QA did not formally escalate schedule slippage; no risk acceptance document generated.
- Indicator: When technical controls are dependent on IT project delivery, there is no adequate escalation mechanism to force resolution or document accepted risk.
Pattern B: Effectiveness check inadequacy
- CAPA-2024-0097-EFF declared effective based on activity completion, not technical control confirmation.
- CAPA-2025-0038-EFF noted "residual risk" but did not trigger re-escalation.
- Root: Effectiveness check templates focus on activity completion checkboxes rather than outcome verification.
- Indicator: Systematic weakness in how effectiveness is defined and measured across the CAPA portfolio.
Pattern C: Management review insufficient to catch systemic gaps
- Monthly management review received consistent "in progress" status on delayed items.
- No formal risk acceptance documentation generated for extended gaps.
- Meeting minutes show awareness but no formal escalation for items at medium-high residual risk.
- Root: Review process lacks decision criteria that differentiate "in progress and on track" from "in progress with acceptable delay" from "in progress with unacceptable risk requiring formal acceptance."
Pattern D: KPI structure driving behavior
- Throughput-based KPI: confirmed by regulatory observer and internal interview data.
- Site committed to remedy in 2024; not remedied by batch CF-882 event in 2025.
- Root: KPI redesign requires cross-functional leadership alignment; was deprioritized during ERP migration period.
Pattern E: Repeat verbal handover failure
- First verbal handover failure: CAPA-2024-0097.
- Second verbal handover failure: batch CF-882 (2025).
- Interval: approximately 15 months.
- Process controls added after 2024 event did not prevent recurrence.
- Root: Written handover requirement added to SOP but no system enforcement; behavioral culture continued from prior practice; no behavioral consequence for verbal carry-forward.
